# ADR-Reporting-Documentatiion
Pharmacovigilance adr reporting and documentation exercise based on a simulated case scenario


## Task 2 – Adverse Drug Reaction Reporting

### Objective

This project demonstrates the documentation and reporting of a suspected Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) using a simulated pharmacovigilance case scenario.

## Patient Information

- Patient Initials: RP
- Age: 32 years
- Gender: Male
- Weight: 65 kg
- Diagnosis: Acute Bacterial Sinusitis
- Past History: No history of allergy
- Family History: No family history of diabetes or hypertension

## Suspected Medication

- Medicine: Amoxicillin
- Dose: 500 mg
- Route: Oral
- Frequency: TID
- Therapy Started: 01/03/2026
- Therapy Stopped: 03/03/2026
- Reason for Use: Acute Bacterial Sinusitis
- Manufacturer: Not known
- Batch/Lot Number: Not known
- Expiry Date: Not known

## Adverse Drug Reaction

- Date of Reaction Onset: 03/03/2026
- Date of Recovery: 06/03/2026
- Reaction: Generalized itching and skin rashes over chest
- Outcome: Recovered completely
- Seriousness: Non-serious

## Management of ADR

Amoxicillin was discontinued on the same day the reaction occurred. The patient was treated with antihistamines and recovered completely by 06/03/2026.

## Additional Information

The patient was receiving Amoxicillin for acute bacterial sinusitis. No previous history of allergy was reported. The suspected medicine was discontinued following the development of the reaction.

## Pharmacovigilance Significance

This case demonstrates the basic process of identifying, documenting, and reporting a suspected adverse drug reaction. Accurate documentation of the suspected medicine, reaction, onset date, outcome, and management is important for pharmacovigilance and patient safety.
## Disclaimer

This is a simulated educational case created for pharmacovigilance learning and ADR reporting practice. It does not represent a real patient case.
